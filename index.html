Why this problem

Immune cell count isn't the same as immune cell reach.

Traditional pathology reports how many CD8+ T-cells are present in a tumor sample. But a T-cell 300 μm from the nearest tumor cell, walled off by cancer-associated fibroblasts, cannot kill that cell — proximity, not just presence, determines whether the immune system can actually engage the tumor.

Importance

A leading cause of skin-cancer death

Melanoma causes the large majority of skin-cancer deaths despite being a minority of skin-cancer cases, and immunotherapy response varies widely — much of that variance is currently invisible to standard cell-count pathology.

Tractability

The imaging exists — the analysis layer doesn't

Multiplexed immunofluorescence (mIF) imaging is already produced in research and increasingly clinical pipelines. Nearest-neighbour spatial statistics are well-established math (Diggle's G-function) — what's missing is a fast, interpretable layer that turns coordinates into a clinician-facing readout.

Neglectedness

Spatial tools exist, actionable ones don't

Research tools like Squidpy compute spatial statistics for computational biologists. Few translate that output into a plain, auditable recommendation a practicing oncologist can use without a bioinformatics background.

The math

What the tool is actually computing.

Every step below is one sentence of math, deliberately — auditability is a feature, not a limitation.

1. Nearest-neighbour distance

For every CD8+ T-cell, find the Euclidean distance to the closest malignant melanocyte. This is the classic point-pattern "G-function" nearest-neighbour statistic (Diggle, 2003).

d(Ti) = minj ‖Ti − Mj‖₂

Computed with a KD-tree (SciPy cKDTree) for O(n log n) queries instead of an O(n·m) brute-force loop over every T-cell/melanocyte pair.

2. Standard deviation of NN distance

The mean tells you typical proximity; the standard deviation tells you consistency. Two tumors can share a mean but differ sharply in whether exclusion is uniform or patchy.

σ = √( Σ(di − d̄)² / n )

A high σ alongside a moderate mean often signals a mixed tumor — some regions infiltrated, others walled off — which a single average would hide.

3. Density vs. distribution

Distribution is where the points are — the raw scatter. Density is how concentrated they are in a given area — a smoothed 2D histogram. Two tumors can have T-cells spread across the same footprint (same distribution) but with very different local clustering (different density) — that difference is invisible in a scatter plot and only shows up as a heatmap.

Fig. — Raw point distribution: thin-tumor T-cells sit close to the tumor edge; thick-tumor T-cells are pushed outward.
Fig. — Same two cases as a density heatmap: exclusion is visible as a dark ring around the tumor mass in the thick-tumor case.

4. Correlation with clinical variables

Per-patient mean NN distance is correlated against Breslow depth (Pearson r, with Spearman's ρ reported as a non-parametric robustness check given the right-skewed Breslow distribution) and against the simulated survival outcome.

Where this goes next

  • Extend beyond CD8+/melanocyte pairs to a multi-cell-type spatial graph (Tregs, TAMs, CAFs) for a fuller immune-exclusion picture.
  • Validate the 50 μm engagement threshold empirically rather than adopting it directly from the literature.
  • Integrate with QuPath's cell-segmentation output so the pipeline can run directly on raw mIF slides, not pre-extracted coordinates.
  • Extend the same spatial framework to other solid tumors where T-cell exclusion is prognostically relevant (e.g. pancreatic, colorectal).
Honesty check

What this isn't, yet.

01

Synthetic, not real, patient data. Cell coordinates and outcomes were generated from a literature-grounded simulation, not extracted from real HTAN/TCGA-SKCM biopsies. Correlation strength on real data will differ.

02

The survival relationship is weak and not significant here (p = 0.44). Reported transparently rather than tuned away — a real validation cohort with more patients and longer follow-up is needed before this metric could inform survival prediction.

03

The 50 μm threshold is drawn from the literature review, not independently validated against outcomes in this dataset or any real cohort.

04

No cell segmentation step. The pipeline assumes pre-extracted cell coordinates and types; it does not yet handle raw microscopy images (that requires QuPath or equivalent segmentation, out of scope for this weekend).

05

Two cell types only. Real melanoma TME involves CAFs, TAMs, Tregs, and more (see literature review) — this tool isolates one interaction for tractability, not because it's the whole picture.
